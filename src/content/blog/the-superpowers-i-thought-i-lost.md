---
title: "The Superpowers I Thought I Lost"
description: "How the effortless wins of my early life faded, and what a friend's blunt honesty taught me about bar raising."
pubDate: 2026-11-14
---

For most of my life, things came easily to me. Grades, honors, and gym gains: I got most of what I wanted with minimal effort. My parents noticed, and they started convincing themselves, and me, that I was some kind of genius.

I am 24 now, and over the past seven years, things have started to change. The so-called natural abilities stopped working as I kept progressing in life. Things got hard. A two-hour study session suddenly needed to be a four-hour grind, and my three-day-a-week workout plan was no longer enough for my gym goals. It felt like I had lost my superpowers. Self-doubt crept in. I even started hating myself for not being able to deliver. I almost gave up.

One night, an old friend called. I was out on a walk to clear my head, and I started venting to him about my issues. Instead of consoling me, he said, "Yeah, yeah... you're a restless person. You always find something new to complain about." Silence followed for five to ten seconds while I processed this truth bomb.

Reflecting on what he said, I finally realized that I had started wanting much bigger things than before: from community and state-level titles to national and international ones, from aiming for a public university in Egypt to studying abroad in the US, and from simply wanting to be fit to working toward a six-pack. "Well, it makes complete sense that I need to put in more effort to get into a US college than to earn a country ranking in ninth grade," I thought to myself.

My ceiling for my own expectations kept rising. My community kept changing, too. As my circle upgraded, the superpowers faded. Nearly everyone around me had them, so they no longer stood out. Self-centered as I was, I ended up blaming myself for losing them, without paying attention to the changing environment or my own patterns of thinking.

I have since learned to recognize this pattern: raising the bar, not giving myself time to celebrate, and always chasing the next thing. I try to stay vigilant about it now. I will probably always be the "restless" person my friend described, but I will make sure to give myself proper rest now and then, even when I haven't fully reached the goals I set. I will also celebrate the "genius" title my parents gave me, even though I'm not really one :)
