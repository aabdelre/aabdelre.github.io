## The Wrong Choice, or So I Thought

In 2017, I got accepted to a high school for gifted students in Egypt. It wasn't quite Xavier's Academy for Mutants—more like a sanctuary for nerds who did well in school and had above-average IQs. The credential felt nice, but I didn't want to go. I didn't want to leave my parents; it was a boarding school. Plus, the school operated on its own system with no official textbooks, and exams were apparently "a free game" based on pre-defined learning outcomes. And just to raise the stakes, I had to shift from an Arabic-based curriculum to an English-based one after spending my entire life barely able to hold a two-turn conversation in English.

I _hated_ my first semester. Homesickness (though I had no real concept of what that meant), electricity and water outages, terrible food, disappointing grades (shocking for someone who'd always had perfect scores), and borderline bullying left me completely miserable. I was ready to transfer—my parents had already agreed because they saw how broken I'd become.

## The 4 AM Revelation

Near the end of that semester, on a random 4 a.m. winter night, I was leaving my dorm to meet classmates for a project due that day. That's when I witnessed something strange: dozens of upperclassmen surrounding someone (let's call him "Mini") on the school's yard. Spoiled food scraps were being thrown at him. Water was being sprayed from a hose. They were chasing him whenever he tried to escape. The confusing part? He was smiling. Genuinely smiling.

Five minutes later, I learned this was a celebration. Ten minutes after that, I found out why: Mini had gotten into **MIT**.

To put this in perspective: I didn't even know what MIT was. But I was intrigued. I spent the rest of my day researching MIT and studying in the United States in general. I completely stopped paying attention to the project that was due (sorry, colleagues—we still got an A+).

## A World I Didn't Know Existed

The idea of studying abroad wasn't even in the realm of possibilities for me at that time. My socioeconomic status, my network, my access to opportunities—none of it was supposed to lead there. Yet here I was, suddenly in an environment where students actually pursued that dream. Where it was _possible_.

Something shifted in me that night. I stopped only seeing this place's flaws—the homesickness, the food, the grades, the bullying. I started seeing what it actually cultivated: independence, discipline, resilience, a sense of community. I found my people, which made the entire experience infinitely better.

When my parents asked if I still wanted to transfer, I said: "Nah, I think I can keep going a bit more."

## The Power of Environment

Mini became my inspiration. And the place that nurtured Mini—that same place I'd wanted to escape—became my own incubator. It was around then that I started truly understanding how much credit we should give to our environment for our achievements. The community that gathered these individuals and created space for them to push each other had an enormous impact on Mini's success, and on mine.

Sometimes all it takes is witnessing one person break through what you thought were your limits to realize those limits were never yours in the first place.
