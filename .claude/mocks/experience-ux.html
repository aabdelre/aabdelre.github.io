Experience section: three directions

Each option keeps the same content (four roles plus education) and changes how a visitor moves through it. Mocks are simplified; colors and fonts follow the site.

OPTION A · ROUTE

A path across the map

Each role is a stop on a dashed route, in the same visual language as the map. The route runs from the earliest role to the latest, and a visitor steps along it with Previous and Next, or taps a stop.

01 Hamilton, BA
┄┄▶
02 HPE intern
┄┄▶
03 Meta
┄┄▶
04 Stealth CTO
  • Ties Experience to the map you already built, so the section feels like part of the same world.
  • Works well on phones, since each stop fits a screen.
  • Harder to scan for a recruiter who wants every role at once.
OPTION B · GIT LOG

A commit history

Experience reads as a git log: each role is a commit with a short hash, its dates, and a one-line message. Clicking a commit expands its details, and a small branch line shows the career path.

a91f2c0Co-Founder & CTO, Stealth Startup HEAD
7b03e4dSoftware Engineer, ML Infra, Meta
3c8e1a7Software Engineer Intern, HPE
  • Fits the terminal identity of the homepage and the existing "git log" heading.
  • Very recognisable to engineers; the metaphor can feel gimmicky to some visitors.
  • Shows every role at once, so it is easy to scan.
OPTION C · NOTEBOOK

A lab notebook, like Research

Experience uses the same notebook layout as Research: a list of roles on the left, and the chosen role written up as numbered cells on the right. Research and Experience then feel like two chapters of one notebook.

$ ls ./experience
Meta
HPE
Stealth
[1] the role
Software Engineer, ML Infrastructure
[2] impact
Cut model-iteration latency about 88%.
  • Consistent with the Research notebook, so the whole site uses one interaction model.
  • Gives room for impact bullets as numbered cells, which suits Meta-level detail.
  • Adds one more layout to learn, and it can feel heavier than a simple list.

Recommendation

I would start with Option C. Experience and Research are the two halves of your technical story, and matching them makes the site feel designed as a whole. Option A is the best second choice if you want more of a journey feel; Option B is fun, but it is a stronger personality choice and may not suit every visitor.

Questions to decide before building: Should Education stay inside Experience or become its own section? Do you want a per-role photo or diagram? Should the route or notebook open in the full-screen takeover, like the other sections?
